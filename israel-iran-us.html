🌍 World Affairs · 2023–2026

Israel, the US & Iran

A detailed, friendly guide to one of the world's biggest news stories — explained just for you, Avyukth!

🇮🇱 Israel 🇺🇸 United States 🇮🇷 Iran
💡

Think of it like this: Imagine three neighbours on the same street who don't get along. One neighbour (Iran) keeps saying mean things and secretly gives money and weapons to troublemakers nearby who bother another neighbour (Israel). The third neighbour (the US) is Israel's best friend and keeps telling Iran to stop. Things kept getting louder and louder — until one day fighting actually broke out. That's what this story is about.

📅 The History

How did this all start? The full story.

This is a long story that goes back many decades. Here are every important moment:

1948
Israel is born 🌟
After World War 2, where millions of Jewish people were killed by Nazi Germany, the United Nations agreed to create a new country called Israel in the Middle East as a homeland for Jewish people. Many Arab countries nearby were unhappy and attacked Israel immediately. Israel survived and has been defending itself ever since.
1948–1978
Iran and Israel were actually friends!
Hard to believe, but for 30 years Iran and Israel had friendly relations. Iran was ruled by a King called Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, who got along well with Israel. Both countries traded, shared intelligence, and cooperated. Iran even supplied oil to Israel during this period.
1979
Iran's Islamic Revolution — everything changes 🔄
A religious leader called Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini led a revolution, removed the King, and took over Iran. The new government was deeply religious and hostile. They immediately declared Israel an enemy, calling it a "Zionist regime" that had no right to exist. They also called America "the Great Satan." That 30-year friendship was instantly destroyed and has never recovered.
1982
Iran creates Hezbollah in Lebanon 🛡️
Iran helped create a new armed group called Hezbollah in Lebanon — the country directly north of Israel. Iran trained them, paid them, and gave them weapons. Hezbollah's main purpose was to fight Israel. This was Iran's first big "proxy" — a group that fights on Iran's behalf without Iran fighting directly.
1988
Hamas is founded in Gaza 🏘️
Hamas was founded in Gaza (a small strip of land next to southern Israel) during a period of Palestinian protests. Hamas believed Israel should be destroyed and replaced with an Islamic state. Over time, Iran began funding and arming Hamas too. Hamas and Israel fought several wars in 2008, 2012, 2014, and 2021, but nothing as big as what came next.
2002–2015
Iran's secret nuclear programme is discovered ☢️
In 2002 it was revealed that Iran had been secretly building nuclear facilities for years. The world was alarmed. The US and many other countries imposed heavy "sanctions" (trade bans and banking restrictions) on Iran to try to make it stop. In 2015, after long negotiations, Iran signed the JCPOA deal — agreeing to limit its nuclear work in exchange for sanctions relief. But in 2018, US President Donald Trump pulled out of the deal, saying it was not strong enough.
7 October 2023
Hamas launches the deadliest attack on Israel 💥
At 6:30am on a Saturday morning — a Jewish holiday — Hamas launched a massive surprise attack on Israel. Over 6,000 fighters broke through the fence from Gaza in 119 places. They fired more than 4,300 rockets, attacked 21 towns, and killed approximately 1,200 people — the deadliest day for Jewish people since the Holocaust. They also took 251 people as hostages into Gaza tunnels. Israel declared war the next day.
October 2023 – Present
Israel fights back in Gaza and across the region ⚡
Israel launched a massive military response into Gaza to destroy Hamas. Meanwhile, Iran's other proxy groups also joined the fight — Hezbollah from Lebanon fired rockets into northern Israel, Houthi rebels in Yemen fired missiles at Israeli cities, and Iran-backed militias in Iraq and Syria attacked US bases. Israel found itself fighting on multiple fronts at once.
1 April 2024
Israel bombs the Iranian embassy in Syria 🏛️💥
Israel launched airstrikes on an Iranian diplomatic building (consulate) in Damascus, Syria. The attack killed seven senior officers of the IRGC (Iran's elite military force), including General Mohammad Reza Zahedi. Iran was furious. Attacking an embassy is considered extremely serious under international law, because embassies are supposed to be protected safe zones. Iran promised to retaliate directly.
13–14 April 2024 — "True Promise I"
Iran attacks Israel directly — a historic first! 😲
For the first time ever in history, Iran launched a direct military attack on Israel — firing more than 300 drones and missiles. The attack was called "Operation True Promise." It was a massive moment. However, Israel (with help from the USA, UK, France, Jordan and even Saudi Arabia) managed to shoot down over 99% of the projectiles in the air before they reached Israel. Only minor damage occurred. The world let out a huge sigh of relief.
July–October 2024
Israel assassinates major Hamas and Hezbollah leaders 🎯
Israel carried out a series of targeted killings of key enemy leaders. In July 2024, Hamas military commander Mohammed Deif was killed in a strike in Gaza. On 31 July, Hamas political chief Ismail Haniyeh — the man who planned the October 7 attack — was assassinated in Tehran, the capital of Iran itself. Then in September 2024, Hezbollah's leader Hassan Nasrallah, who had led the group for over 30 years, was killed in a massive Israeli strike in Beirut, Lebanon. Iran and its allies were severely shaken.
1 October 2024 — "True Promise II"
Iran launches a second direct missile attack 🚀
Iran retaliated for the assassination of Haniyeh and Nasrallah by firing approximately 200 ballistic missiles directly at Israel. Israel's missile defence systems, including the famous "Iron Dome" and "Arrow" interceptors, destroyed most of them. Some missiles hit open ground. Israel vowed to respond.
October 2024
Israel strikes Iran's air defences 🛡️
Israel retaliated by striking Iran's air defence systems — the radar and missile batteries that protect Iran's skies. Israel destroyed several of Iran's Russian-supplied S-300 missile defence systems near Isfahan and Tehran. This was a clever move: by destroying Iran's defences, Israel was preparing for potentially bigger strikes in the future.
November 2024 – May 2025
Diplomacy — the USA tries to negotiate ☎️
US President Donald Trump (who won the 2024 election) tried to negotiate a new nuclear deal with Iran. Talks were held in Oman and Rome. In May 2025, Iran's nuclear watchdog (the IAEA) reported that Iran now had enough enriched uranium to potentially build 9 nuclear weapons. The IAEA also declared Iran "non-compliant" — breaking its nuclear agreements — for the first time since 2005. The talks were running out of time.
13 June 2025 — "Operation Rising Lion"
Israel launches the 12-Day War ⚔️
At 3:15am, Israel launched a massive surprise attack on Iran — Operation Rising Lion. Over 200 Israeli fighter jets dropped more than 330 munitions on roughly 100 targets across Iran, including nuclear facilities at Natanz, air bases, military sites, and radar systems. Israel also assassinated several top Iranian military generals and nuclear scientists. It was the most severe attack on Iran since the Iran-Iraq War of the 1980s. Iran's S-300 air defences — already weakened in the October 2024 strikes — were mostly powerless to stop the jets.
13–22 June 2025 — "True Promise III"
Iran fights back with 550+ missiles and 1,000+ drones 🚀
Iran retaliated with Operation True Promise III — its largest ever counter-attack. Over 12 days, Iran fired more than 550 ballistic missiles and over 1,000 suicide drones at Israeli cities. Missiles struck neighbourhoods in Tel Aviv and Haifa, damaging apartment buildings. Israelis spent days in bomb shelters. Israel's multi-layered defence systems — Iron Dome (close range), David's Sling (medium), and Arrow (long range) — intercepted most missiles, but Israel was stretched hard. Over the 12 days, Israel carried out close to 360 strikes across 27 of Iran's provinces.
21–22 June 2025
The USA enters the war 🇺🇸💣
The USA entered the war directly. US bombers dropped special "bunker-buster" bombs on Iran's three most fortified nuclear sites: Natanz, Fordow, and Isfahan. Fordow was built deep underground inside a mountain and only the USA's most powerful bombs could reach it. Iran then fired missiles at Al-Udeid Air Base in Qatar, the largest US military base in the Middle East.
24 June 2025
Ceasefire — the 12 days end 🕊️
After intense mediation by the small country of Oman, a ceasefire was agreed. By the end of the 12 days: Iran reported at least 610 of its citizens killed; Israel reported 28 killed. Iran's nuclear programme was severely damaged. Israel had destroyed 70 of Iran's air defence batteries. But Iran's nuclear capability was not completely eliminated — the IAEA later said Iran could enrich uranium again within months.
28 February 2026
The conflict reignites — and gets even bigger 💥
After the ceasefire broke down and new nuclear negotiations failed, Israel and the US launched coordinated strikes on Iran again on 28 February 2026. This time, the opening strike assassinated Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei in Tehran. Iran retaliated by launching missiles across nine countries and — critically — began blocking the Strait of Hormuz, the world's most important oil shipping route. This is where things stand as of March 2026.
🕵️ Iran's Proxy Network

Iran's "Axis of Resistance" — the groups it funds

Iran doesn't always fight directly. Instead it gives money, weapons and training to armed groups in other countries to fight on its behalf. These are called "proxies." Here are the main ones:

🇵🇸
Hamas
📍 Gaza Strip, next to Israel
Founded 1988. Iran funds and arms Hamas with rockets, drones, and money. Hamas launched the October 7 2023 attack, killing ~1,200 Israelis. Israel has been trying to destroy Hamas ever since.
🇱🇧
Hezbollah
📍 Lebanon, north of Israel
Iran's most powerful proxy. Created in 1982, armed with over 100,000 rockets. Fired at Israel after October 7. Their longtime leader Hassan Nasrallah was killed by Israel in September 2024 — a huge blow.
🇾🇪
Houthis
📍 Yemen (across the Red Sea)
Houthi rebels who control northern Yemen. After October 7, they began firing missiles at Israel and attacking international shipping in the Red Sea, disrupting global trade. The USA and UK launched airstrikes on them in response.
🇮🇶
Kataib Hezbollah & Iraqi militias
📍 Iraq and Syria
Iran-backed armed groups in Iraq and Syria. They launched many drone and rocket attacks on US military bases in the region, killing American soldiers. The US struck back multiple times.
🧩
The "Axis of Resistance"
Iran calls all these groups together the "Axis of Resistance." The idea is that they all work together to resist (fight against) Israel and American influence in the Middle East. By October 2024, Israel had severely weakened all of these groups — Hamas was partly destroyed, Hezbollah's leader was killed, and the Houthis were under US and UK bombardment.
☢️ The Nuclear Question

What is a nuclear bomb — and why is everyone scared?

🔬

Super simple science: Inside every tiny atom is incredible energy. When scientists learned how to release this energy, they found it could either make electricity (useful and safe) or create a terrifyingly powerful bomb. One nuclear bomb dropped on Hiroshima, Japan in 1945 destroyed an entire city in seconds and killed over 70,000 people. That is why everyone is so scared of nuclear weapons.

🛢️ Trade & The Strait of Hormuz

The world's most important oil bottleneck

🚰

Think of it like a tap: Imagine one single tap that supplies water to 20 out of every 100 houses on Earth. If someone turns that tap off, 20 families have no water. The Strait of Hormuz is that tap — but for oil. It is a narrow strip of sea between Iran and Oman, only about 40 km wide at its narrowest point. Almost all the oil from the world's biggest oil-producing countries passes through it.

🗺️ The Strait of Hormuz — why it matters

Oil from Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, UAE, Qatar, Iraq and Iran all flows through this one narrow gap before reaching the rest of the world.

🇮🇳
Why India is especially affected
India and China together receive 44% of all oil exported through the Strait of Hormuz. India buys huge amounts of oil from Saudi Arabia, UAE, Iraq and Iran — all of which ships through this strait. If it closes, India faces an energy crisis. In 2026 when Iran began blocking the strait, India sent Navy destroyers to escort Indian-owned tankers through the Gulf of Oman.
🚨
What's happening right now (March 2026)
After the February 2026 strikes by Israel and the US, Iran's Revolutionary Guard began blocking the Strait of Hormuz and attacking ships. Oil prices surged above $100 per barrel. Major shipping companies like Maersk suspended transits. The US military launched operations to reopen the strait. This is the most serious disruption to global oil since the 1970s oil crisis.
🎯 Goals

What does each country want?

🇮🇱 Israel wants…
Iran to never build a nuclear bomb — Israel fears it could be destroyed
Iran to stop funding Hamas, Hezbollah, and other proxy groups
The hostages from October 7 to come home
Safety and security for its 9 million people
🇺🇸 USA wants…
No nuclear weapons in the Middle East — could trigger a world war
To protect its ally Israel and its military bases in the region
The Strait of Hormuz to stay open for global trade
Its soldiers in the region to be safe from Iranian proxy attacks
🇮🇷 Iran wants…
To be the most powerful country in the Middle East
Sanctions (trade bans) lifted so its economy can recover
The USA and Israel to stay out of its affairs
Nuclear technology — though it says only for peaceful purposes
📖 Key words

Words you'll hear on the news

🏛️
Sanctions
Like a collective punishment from other countries — the USA told banks and businesses around the world "don't trade with Iran, don't let them use your financial system." This made Iran's economy very weak, its currency worthless, and everyday prices in Iran very high for ordinary people.
🤝
Proxy groups
Iran doesn't always fight directly — it gives money, weapons and training to armed groups like Hamas and Hezbollah to fight on its behalf. They're called "proxies" — like hiring someone else to fight your battles. This way Iran can cause trouble without technically starting a war itself.
☢️
Nuclear enrichment
The process of making uranium (a special metal found underground) more and more powerful. At 5% it powers electricity stations safely. At 90%+ it can power a nuclear bomb. Iran had enriched uranium to 60% by 2025 — far beyond what any electricity programme needs, which is why Israel and the US were so alarmed.
🛡️
Iron Dome / David's Sling / Arrow
Israel has three layers of missile defence. Iron Dome shoots down short-range rockets (used against Hamas rockets). David's Sling handles medium-range missiles. Arrow 2 and Arrow 3 intercept long-range ballistic missiles from countries like Iran. Together they form a multi-layer "onion" defence that made it possible to survive Iran's massive missile barrages in 2024 and 2025.
🪖
IRGC (Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps)
Iran's elite military force — like a special army that is also responsible for spreading Iran's revolutionary ideology abroad. The IRGC runs Iran's missile programme, nuclear programme, and manages all the proxy groups like Hezbollah and Hamas. It is one of the most powerful institutions in Iran.
🇮🇳 India's connection

Why does this matter to India?

🛢️
Oil prices go up
India imports about 85% of its oil. Much of it comes from the Middle East through the Strait of Hormuz. When there's conflict, oil prices rise — meaning petrol in India gets more expensive, trucks and factories cost more to run, and everything from food to medicines becomes pricier.
👨‍👩‍👧
80,000+ Indians live in Israel
A large number of Indian workers, students and families live in Israel. When war breaks out, India's government has to work urgently to keep them safe, help them evacuate, and arrange emergency flights home.
🚢
Ships and trade routes
Ships carrying Indian goods to Europe pass through the Red Sea and near Yemen, where the Houthis have been attacking vessels. Many ships now have to go all the way around Africa instead — adding weeks to the journey and making Indian exports and imports more expensive.
⚖️
India's diplomatic balancing act
India has good relations with both Israel (a technology and defence partner) and Iran (India buys oil from Iran and they share a trade route to Central Asia called the International North-South Transport Corridor). India tries hard not to take sides — it needs both relationships.
